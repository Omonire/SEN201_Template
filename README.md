## Project description

A publicly distributable collection of **105 independently usable AI prompts** for the SEN 201 Software Engineering assignment.

Each prompt is a complete, self-contained instruction set that a student can copy into ChatGPT, Claude or another capable AI platform. Every prompt ships with its own predefined case study, requires genuine online research, enforces an explicit student approval gate before any document is created and specifies two separate editable DOCX deliverables.

> **These are prompts, not completed assignments.** No assignment content, student record or generated document is stored in this repository.

Created by Omonire Great (GR8), a voluntary academic support initiative for Software Engineering students.

GitHub username: `Omonire`

---

## Purpose

The library exists to help SEN 201 students understand how software development models are selected and applied, and to give them a consistent, academically sound starting point for their assignment. The prompts standardise the required academic components while varying case studies, instructional structures and analytical emphasis so that students can choose a brief that fits their own learning needs.

## SDLC models covered

Exactly seven model folders, **15 prompts each**:

| # | Folder | Model | Focus |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | `01-WATERFALL-MODEL` | Waterfall Model | Stable, sequential, sign-off driven |
| 2 | `02-V-MODEL` | V-Model (Verification and Validation) | Development paired with testing |
| 3 | `03-SPIRAL-MODEL` | Spiral Model | Risk analysis every cycle |
| 4 | `04-PROTOTYPE-MODEL` | Prototype Model | Requirement discovery via feedback |
| 5 | `05-AGILE-MODEL` | Agile Model | Adaptation and frequent delivery |
| 6 | `06-INCREMENTAL-MODEL` | Incremental Model | Functional slices added in sequence |
| 7 | `07-DEVOPS-MODEL` | DevOps Model | Continuous integration, delivery and monitoring |

Each folder contains `prompt-01.md` through `prompt-15.md`.

## How to select and copy a prompt

1. Pick the SDLC model you are studying or that your lecturer assigned.
2. Open the matching model folder and scan the case study titles in the prompt headings.
3. Open one `prompt-NN.md` and copy **the entire file**.
4. Paste it as the first message in a new AI conversation.
5. Follow the stages exactly and supply your own student details when asked.
6. Review the proposal at Stage 6. Approve it explicitly only when satisfied.
7. Draw your own diagram in draw.io, verify it and insert the screenshot and live link.

> No scenario is reused. Do not substitute a different case study — each one is deliberately matched to its model.

## The two DOCX deliverables

Every prompt requires two separate, editable Word documents **after approval**.

| Document | File name pattern | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Main assignment** | `SEN201_<Model>_Assignment.docx` | The coursework itself: title page, student details, introduction, model definition, SDLC overview, selected model, case study, application, justification, advantages, diagram explanation, screenshot and URL placeholders, disadvantages, conclusion and references |
| **Explanation guide** | `SEN201_<Model>_Explanation_Guide.docx` | A tutor-style companion: section meanings, why the scenario was chosen, how the model applies, why advantages and disadvantages matter, diagram interpretation, draw.io steps, terminology, likely lecturer questions and strong sample answers |

The guide **complements** the assignment. It does not repeat it word for word.

## Online research requirements

Every prompt requires the AI to research online before preparing anything. It must:

- Search for academic and technical material.
- Prioritise textbooks, peer-reviewed publications, university resources, official documentation and reputable Software Engineering publications.
- Verify titles, authors, dates and URLs.
- Cross-check important claims.
- Use in-text citations and a formatted reference list.
- **Never invent** publications, authors, DOIs, URLs, dates or case-study evidence.

If browsing is unavailable, the AI must disclose that and ask how to proceed rather than pretending research occurred.

## Student approval process

The gate is strict and identical in principle across all 105 prompts:

| Stage | Action |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Collect student information |
| 2 | Present the model and predefined scenario |
| 3 | Conduct online research |
| 4 | Propose an academic title |
| 5 | Present outline, analysis, findings and references |
| 6 | Ask the student to review |
| **7** | **WAIT for explicit approval** |

An acceptable approval statement:

> I have reviewed the proposed structure and research. I approve. You may proceed with document generation.

Silence, ordinary acknowledgements and ambiguous replies are **not** approval. If changes are requested, the proposal is revised and approval requested again. No DOCX is generated before explicit approval.

## draw.io instructions

The structural diagram is compulsory. draw.io is a free browser-based diagramming tool at https://app.diagrams.net/ .

Each prompt explains what draw.io is, why it suits Software Engineering diagrams, the exact diagram for that model, the shapes, labels, arrows and connections, and step-by-step drawing guidance.

The student creates the diagram manually and places the screenshot and live link here:

```text
[INSERT YOUR DRAW.IO STRUCTURAL DIAGRAM SCREENSHOT HERE]

[INSERT YOUR DRAW.IO LIVE DIAGRAM LINK HERE]
```

> The AI must not claim it created the diagram. The student verifies it before submission.

## Academic integrity guidance

These prompts are educational guides, not answer keys. Use them to understand how to approach the assignment, then review, verify, edit and take ownership of everything you submit. Check every technical claim, citation and reference independently. Follow your lecturer's instructions and your institution's academic integrity policies. Where they conflict with this template, **the lecturer wins**.

AI-generated content can contain inaccuracies. Do not submit work you do not understand and cannot explain.
